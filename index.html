1. Project Overview

Our project aims to design and develop a robotic claw inspired by the natural gripping mechanism of a falcon. Falcons have strong, curved talons that catch and hold objects securely. We are studying this natural mechanism and applying similar ideas to a robotic system.

The claw will use soft fingers, electronic sensors, and an Arduino microcontroller. Our main goal is to grip different objects while controlling the gripping force. We are currently researching mechanisms and selecting a suitable design.

2. Project Objectives

  1. Design a robotic claw inspired by falcon talons.
  2. Develop soft fingers that bend in a curved shape.
  3. Use Arduino to control the opening and closing of the claw.
  4. Use air pressure to bend the fingers and grip objects.
  5. Integrate a force sensor to measure gripping force.
  6. Design parts and molds in AutoCAD and manufacture them using 3D printing.
  7. Test gripping performance with different objects.

3. Inspiration From Nature

Falcons have powerful claws that help them capture and hold prey. Their talons have a curved shape for effective gripping, strong gripping ability, flexible finger movement, natural tendon-based movement, and the ability to hold objects securely. We want to apply some of these characteristics to our robotic claw.

4. Initial Design Research

We compared designs with jointed fingers and single curved fingers to find a mechanism that is practical to manufacture and control.

Concept 1: Tendon-driven robotic gripper

A motor pulls a tendon wire, causing the finger joints to bend and grip an object. This is interesting because it resembles natural tendon movement.

Main components: motor, pulley, tendon wire, finger joints, rigid links, and base structure.

Concept 2: Bio-inspired claw mechanism

This design uses curved claw structures inspired by animal gripping mechanisms and provides ideas for the shape and movement of our claw.

Concept 3: Soft fiber-reinforced fingers (selected design)

We selected the Fiber-Reinforced Bending Actuator from the Soft Robotics Toolkit. Each finger is a soft silicone tube (bladder) wrapped with strong, non-stretch thread. One side also has a flat, non-stretch layer.

How it works:

  • When air is pumped into the finger, the silicone tries to expand.
  • The thread limits outward expansion so the finger can lengthen.
  • The flat layer prevents one side from lengthening.
  • The other side lengthens, making the finger bend inward around an object.
  • When air is released, the silicone returns toward its original straight shape.

Why we chose it: its curved motion resembles a talon closing around prey; soft silicone can adapt to objects; the fingers return without springs; and the molds can be designed in AutoCAD and 3D printed. Thread placement can also change the motion (bending, extending, or twisting).

Manufacturing challenge: this finger takes longer to make than other soft fingers. Casting can take up to five days, so fabrication needs to be planned early.

6. Proposed Working Principle

  1. Arduino turns on the air pump and opens the inlet solenoid valve.
  2. Air flows through the tubes into the soft fingers.
  3. The thread wrapping and stiff layer make the fingers bend inward.
  4. The claw closes around an object.
  5. The force sensor measures gripping force, and the pressure sensor measures air pressure.
  6. When the required gripping force is reached, Arduino closes the valve and stops adding air.
  7. To release the object, Arduino opens the exhaust valve; air leaves the fingers and they return toward their straight shape.

This proposed mechanism will be evaluated during the design stage.

7. Mechanical Design

We plan to design the robotic claw using AutoCAD. The design will include a supporting base, soft-finger molds, finger mounting points, air-tube routing, pump and valve mounting locations, and a force-sensor mounting position. The base and molds are planned for 3D printing.

Finger manufacturing steps

  1. Design the finger molds in AutoCAD and export them as STL files.
  2. 3D-print the mold pieces.
  3. Cast the first silicone layer and add the flat inextensible layer on one side.
  4. Wrap the thread around the finger.
  5. Cast an outer silicone layer to cover and hold the thread.
  6. Seal the end caps and connect the air tube.
  7. Test the finger for leaks and bending.

8. Arduino Control System

Arduino will be the main controller. It will turn the air pump on and off, open and close the solenoid valves, control the claw, read the pressure and force sensors, monitor gripping force, and help prevent excessive gripping force and pressure. The program will be developed after the final electronic components are selected.

We also aim to measure gripping force with a sensor and control the claw using Arduino. Final performance will depend on the finger design, air pressure, and experimental results.
